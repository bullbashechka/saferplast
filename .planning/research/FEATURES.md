# Feature Landscape

**Domain:** PVC/aluminum windows, doors, balconies, glazing, installation and repair landing page
**Project:** SaFerplast
**Researched:** 2026-03-24
**Overall confidence:** MEDIUM

## Executive Summary

High-converting window and door landing pages consistently optimize for three outcomes: immediate trust, immediate service understanding, and immediate path-to-quote. Across major operators such as Pella, Renewal by Andersen, Window World, and Anglian, the core pattern is stable: a strong first-screen CTA, clear product and service segmentation, visible proof, a simple consultation or quote request, and reassurance around installation, warranty, and financing.

For this domain, users do not expect a full ecommerce experience. They do expect to understand what the company installs, whether the company is credible, roughly what affects price, and how to request a measurement or consultation without friction. A simple calculator is valuable when it helps qualify and educate rather than pretending to generate a final contract-grade estimate.

For SaFerplast, the MVP should bias toward lead capture over sophistication: hero CTA, segmented services, product and material clarity, trust proof, process explanation, FAQ, simple calculator, and a short lead form. Advanced visual tools, rich galleries, financing calculators, and deep interactive configuration are better treated as v2 differentiators unless already available.

## Table Stakes

Features users expect. Missing these makes the landing feel weak or unfinished for this category.

### 1. First-Screen Conversion

| Feature | Why Expected | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|--------------|------------|-------|
| Primary CTA above the fold | Users expect to request a consultation, measurement, or quote immediately | Low | Use one dominant action, not 3 competing equal CTAs |
| Sticky contact actions | Window and door leads often convert after scrolling and comparison | Low | On mobile, phone and WhatsApp should stay reachable |
| Short lead form | Category leaders ask for minimal initial details before quote or consultation | Low | Name, phone, service type, optional comment is enough for first contact |
| Phone and messenger visibility | This is a high-consideration local service; many users want human contact fast | Low | Especially important for urgent repair requests |

### 2. Service Clarity

| Feature | Why Expected | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|--------------|------------|-------|
| Clear service list | Users need to confirm the company handles windows, doors, balconies, repair, glazing, hardware, slopes, and sills | Low | Separate manufacturing, installation, and repair |
| Product category blocks | Visitors expect quick recognition of PVC windows, aluminum systems, doors, balcony glazing, and related services | Low | Cards or sections work better than long paragraphs |
| Material distinction | PVC vs aluminum is a core buyer decision | Low | Explain best-fit use cases, not engineering theory |
| Property-type coverage | Users want to know if the company serves apartments, houses, offices, and commercial sites | Low | This reduces qualification uncertainty |

### 3. Trust and Proof

| Feature | Why Expected | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|--------------|------------|-------|
| Review and testimonial section | Social proof is standard on major competitor sites | Low | Real names, neighborhoods, or project types improve credibility |
| Warranty or guarantee block | Buyers expect reassurance on installation and product quality | Low | Keep terms plain and specific |
| Company differentiators | Users expect to understand why this company vs another installer | Low | In-house production, direct pricing, speed, experience |
| Real project photos | Buyers want evidence of actual work quality | Medium | Even a small curated gallery is better than stock-only imagery |

### 4. Price Discovery

| Feature | Why Expected | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|--------------|------------|-------|
| "From" pricing or price factors | Users want some orientation before submitting | Low | Avoid fake precision; show what changes price |
| Simple calculator | Strong fit for this project and common user expectation for this category | Medium | Should output an estimate range, not a final quote |
| Free measurement or free consultation messaging | Common pattern in replacement-window competitors | Low | Works well as a friction remover and lead magnet |

### 5. Installation Confidence

| Feature | Why Expected | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|--------------|------------|-------|
| Process section | Buyers want to know how consultation, measurement, manufacturing, installation, and handoff work | Low | Usually 3-5 steps |
| Timeline expectations | Users care how fast work starts and how long it takes | Low | Even approximate timing reduces anxiety |
| FAQ | Standard for objections about noise, timing, warranty, pricing, measurement, and materials | Low | Also supports search visibility and sales clarity |

### 6. Local Relevance

| Feature | Why Expected | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|--------------|------------|-------|
| Service area coverage | Users need to know whether their city or region is served | Low | Important for qualification and trust |
| Local contact details | Local phone, address, or production and showroom details increase legitimacy | Low | Particularly useful for brownfield local-service SEO later |

## Differentiators

Useful for stronger conversion or brand separation, but not required for v1.

### 1. Better Qualification and Quote Experience

| Feature | Value Proposition | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|-------------------|------------|-------|
| Multi-step calculator by product type | Gives more relevant estimates for window, door, and balcony jobs | Medium | Best differentiator if kept simple and fast |
| Calculator result capture | Turns estimate intent into a lead with prefilled context | Medium | Good handoff into WhatsApp or form |
| "Send estimate to WhatsApp or Telegram" | Matches project lead-routing model and reduces form abandonment | Medium | Strong fit for this business, but should stay optional |
| Urgent repair CTA path | Separates repair-intent users from full replacement users | Low | Useful because repair urgency differs from planned purchases |

| Downloadable brochure or spec sheet | Supports users who need to compare options offline | Low | More useful for B2B and commercial leads |

### 3. Stronger Visual Proof

| Feature | Value Proposition | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|-------------------|------------|-------|
| Before and after gallery | High-conversion proof for renovation-style services | Medium | Frequently used by premium competitors |

| Video walkthroughs or install clips | Builds trust and perceived professionalism | High | Good differentiator if authentic footage exists |

### 4. Trust Amplifiers

| Feature | Value Proposition | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|-------------------|------------|-------|

| Team or production photos | Supports "own manufacturing" and "direct from manufacturer" positioning | Medium | Strong for price and trust messaging |
| Case-study snippets | Makes claims concrete: scope, timeline, result | Medium | Use a few examples, not a blog-like archive |

### 5. Convenience Features

## Anti-Features

Features to explicitly not prioritize in v1 unless they directly improve lead conversion.

| Anti-Feature | Why Avoid | What to Do Instead |
|--------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Full ecommerce checkout | Window and door jobs are custom-scoped and usually finalized after measurement | Use estimate ranges plus consultation request |
| Over-detailed technical configurator | Too slow and complex for landing traffic | Keep the calculator short and sales-oriented |
| Large blog or content hub in v1 | Adds scope without helping immediate launch conversion | Use a focused FAQ and a few educational comparison blocks |
| CRM-style customer cabinet | Not needed for the first public lead-gen version | Route leads into messaging plus manual follow-up |
| Overloaded mega-page with every service detail | Hurts scanning and CTA focus | Keep sections modular and concise |
| Aggressive popup stack | Damages trust on a high-consideration purchase | Use inline offers and sticky contact options |

## Feature Dependencies

```text
Service clarity -> Trust

Service area coverage -> Local CTA relevance
```

## MVP Recommendation

Prioritize v1 around these concrete features:

1. Hero with one primary CTA plus visible phone and WhatsApp action
2. Service and product category sections covering PVC, aluminum, doors, balconies, and repair
3. Strong trust block with differentiators, warranty, and real reviews
4. Simple calculator that returns an approximate range and pushes into lead capture
5. Short lead form for consultation or free measurement
6. Process section, timeline expectations, and FAQ
7. Service area and local contact proof

Defer to v2:

| Feature | Reason to Defer |
|---------|-----------------|
| Visualizer or design-your-own tool | High effort, not required to validate demand |
| Deep gallery filters and case studies | Valuable, but secondary to basic proof and lead capture |
| Advanced calculator with many permutations | Risk of scope creep and false precision |
| Brochure center or downloadable resources | Helpful but not core to the first conversion path |
| Channel preference routing logic | Useful after lead volume justifies optimization |

## Scope Guidance for SaFerplast

### Recommended v1 feature set
- Hero with consultation, free measurement, and discount framing
- WhatsApp-first contact flow with Telegram as secondary
- Short form and callback option
- Product and service cards for windows, doors, balconies, repair, glazing units, hardware, sills, and slopes
- PVC vs aluminum explanation
- Simple calculator with dimensions plus product type, material, and install or repair intent
- Trust section with in-house production, direct pricing, speed, warranty, and real proofs
- Process section
- FAQ
- Service area block

### Recommended v2 differentiators
- Better estimate flow with result summary and messenger handoff
- Before and after project gallery
- Comparison tables for material and system choices
- Urgent repair shortcut flow
- Production and team credibility section
